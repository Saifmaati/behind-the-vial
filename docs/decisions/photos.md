# Decisions: intro photographs (assets/img/intro-*.webp, tools/img/*)

One line each: decision, then why.

- **Wikimedia Commons file pages plus NCI's own record are the licence evidence; each file page's wikitext and API metadata are snapshotted next to the original in `tools/.cache/photos/<name>/`.** The licence is then checked where it is declared, and the evidence survives the page changing later.
- **NCI Visuals Online no longer resolves (DNS), so its records for #2238 and #2129 were read from the Internet Archive.** Both say "Reuse Restrictions: None - This image is in the public domain", matching the Commons templates.
- **Main vial = NCI "Chemotherapy Vials" (1986, black and white, public domain).** It is the only clean, real photo found of small glass vials with rubber stoppers, crimp seals and flip-off caps and no printed label; every colour single-vial photo under an acceptable licence carries a brand or manufacturer label, which the brief excludes.
- **Second vial picture = "Vial caps" top view (Epolk, CC BY-SA 4.0), kept for colour.** Real flip-off caps, no label or brand visible; its EXIF carried GPS coordinates, which the re-encode drops.
- **These photos must not be captioned as retatrutide or peptide vials.** They show other medicines (NCI: chemotherapy drugs; Epolk: a vaccine kit), so a "this is a peptide vial" caption would be a false claim. The suggested alt text only describes what is visible.
- **Syringe = "Insulin syringe foto" (Rehab Center Parus, CC BY-SA 4.0, VRT-confirmed permission)**, because it is the one licensed photo with orange caps on both ends, as the owner asked; caps on, plunger fully in, empty, no person, no liquid, no brand.
- ~~**The syringe keeps its factory unit scale (10–100)**~~ **Superseded by the fix pass (docs/decisions/fix.md):** the printed numbers and "UNITS" are retouched out of the barrel (`tools/img/retouch-syringe.mjs`), keeping the plain tick marks, because the hard exclusion "tick marks carry no numbers or units" has no photo exception and insulin-syringe units are how gray-market amounts are passed around. The retouch matches the barrel's own shading, so the photo still looks real.
- **Credit reads "Rehab Center Parus" with no link to the clinic's site.** The licence note asks only for the name; a link is "appreciated", not required, and the site must not link to commercial hosts (`tests/commerce.test.mjs`).
- **Shipped credits link only to the CC licence deed and ASSETS.md on github.com; full Commons URLs live in ASSETS.md and docs/assets/photos.md.** CC BY-SA 4.0 allows attribution through a linked credits page, and `commons.wikimedia.org` is not an allowed link host in shipped files.
- **Our crops of the CC BY-SA photos are CC BY-SA 4.0; the NCI-derived files stay public domain.** That is what ShareAlike requires; it does not affect the site's other files.
- **Blood = NCI scanning electron micrograph of normal circulating human blood (Wetzel and Schaefer, 1982, public domain), left in its true black and white.** SEM images have no natural colour; if the intro tints it in CSS, the caption should say "colour added".
- **The 16:9 blood crop is the top of the frame, mostly red cells; the 4:5 crop keeps nearly the whole frame, with two white cells.** The intro line is about blood carrying the drug, and red cells read best; the portrait frame can't avoid the white cells, and they are real.
- **Each picture comes as 16:9 (800/1600 px wide) and 4:5 (640/1280 px wide), named by real pixel width.** The 4:5 versions are 1280 × 1600 because the vial original is only 1800 px tall, and upscaling adds bytes without detail; `srcset` with `w` descriptors handles any widths.
- **The syringe also comes as a transparent cut-out (1600 × 300 and 800 × 150), plus a 4:5 version rotated 45°.** Its background is pure #FFFFFF, so clearing only the white connected to the frame edge gives a clean cut-out that works in dark mode and can be moved with CSS transforms; a horizontal syringe would leave a 4:5 frame mostly empty.
- **The rotation points the capped needle up and to the right, never down at a body.** Pointing down reads as "about to inject"; up-right is a neutral product shot.
- **Encoded with sharp (already in tools/node_modules through @gltf-transform) instead of a headless-Chrome canvas.** It gives Lanczos3 resampling, real WebP encoder settings (quality 82, effort 6, smart subsampling) and drops all metadata by default, and it installs nothing new; quality would step down automatically if a file went over budget, but none needed to.
- **No skin macro was added (optional item).** Nothing tasteful turned up quickly under an acceptable licence, and the body chapters already use our own anatomy renders.
